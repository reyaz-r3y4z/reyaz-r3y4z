how i think

My Way of Thinking

Before I write a single line of code or build anything, I need to understand the core problem — not the surface-level request, but the real underlying issue. The wrong solution to the right problem is something you can fix. The right solution to the wrong problem is wasted time for everyone.

"Understand the problem fully, then bring everything you have to solve it."

I believe the simplest solution is usually the best one. But here is the important part — simple is not easy to find. A lot of analytical work happens in my head before I arrive at something clean. I do the complexity so the solution does not have to carry it. Sometimes the simple answer is technically involved, but if you understand the fundamentals, even that feels natural.

Everything we build in AI and data science is mathematics wearing different clothes. I love maths — not as an abstract subject, but as the language that explains why things work. Understanding the maths underneath means I can reason about models instead of just running them. That is the difference between using a tool and understanding it.

PRINCIPLE 01
Problem First
I never start building until I fully understand what the problem actually is. Not what someone asked for — what they need.
PRINCIPLE 02
Simple is Earned
Clean solutions don't happen by accident. They come after doing the hard analytical work upfront and removing everything that isn't necessary.
PRINCIPLE 03
Maths is the Foundation
I trust the mathematics underneath. Understanding it means I can reason about why something works, not just whether it does.
how i work

The Way I Work

I ask more questions than most people are comfortable with at the start of a project. That is intentional. The clarity I get upfront saves an enormous amount of effort downstream. I would rather spend twenty minutes understanding something properly than two hours building the wrong thing.

I am also honest about what I don't know. If there is a gap in my knowledge, my first instinct is to say so and go fill it. I will not pretend to know something I don't. I go understand it properly, then come back. That is how I have always worked and it is how I teach as well.

HOW 01
Think Before Building
Most of the heavy work happens analytically before I open an editor. I map the solution first, then implement it.
HOW 02
Honest About Gaps
If I don't know something I say so directly, go and understand it, then come back. No guessing. No bluffing.
HOW 03
Teach to Confirm
Teaching is how I test my own understanding. If I cannot explain something clearly to someone else, I am not done learning it yet.
HOW 04
No Overengineering
I start with the minimum thing that works. Complexity is only added when simplicity provably cannot do the job.
why data science · why ai

Why I Chose This Path

I chose this field because I want to be the kind of teacher who gives the computer sense — not just instructions, but genuine understanding. How to think. Why something is true. What is ethical. How to arrive at an answer safely and responsibly.

I want to transfer what I know into code, into models, into systems — so those systems can one day help someone else learn. Whether that is a child making sense of mathematics, or a team making sense of complex data, the goal is the same: knowledge that travels forward.

Mathematics and computers are the same thing to me. Both are languages for expressing truth. I want machines to speak that language well. And I want to be part of the team that teaches them.

"I don't just want to use AI. I want to be someone who shapes how it thinks."

That is why Maincode's work interests me. Building AI that understands context, earns trust, and is held to a higher standard — that is the kind of work I want to be part of. Not just shipping models, but getting the thinking right.
